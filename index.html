Design system

Surfaces you could press.

Niveus is a tactile, adaptive interface language. Controls are bevelled keys with light falling across them, fields are wells cut into the surface, and glass is kept for the chrome that floats over everything else. The same components carry their hierarchy from light to dark, because nothing below is defined in terms of an appearance — only in terms of a role.

Color

The whole system has one input. Set a brand color and the accent, its hover and press states, the text that lands on it, the focus ring, the tint in the greys and the lean of the status colors are all re-derived — in both appearances, with nothing else to set. Try it: the page below is not reloading, and no component knows it happened.

#4f6cff
A link, too

The label on a filled control is derived, not chosen: below a lightness threshold it resolves to near-white, above it to near-black — so the yellow brand gets dark labels and the violet one light ones. The color is also nudged away from the middle of the range until its own label clears 4.5:1, which is why a mid-tone green arrives slightly deeper than the swatch you picked.

One page, several brands

data-nv-brand re-derives the set for a subtree, so a region can carry its own color without a second theme and without leaving the system.

Continuity

Every change should read as a continuation of the state before it. When something moves, grows, collapses or changes appearance, the change itself should be perceptible — where it came from, where it went, and why. The goal is not to animate everything; it is that nothing pops into a new state the user could have watched it reach.

Moving through one surface

Switching tabs should feel like moving along a surface, not replacing the interface. The panels travel in the direction you are going — forward to the right, back to the left — so the strip has a geography you can remember.

Activity

The outgoing panel leaves towards where you came from while the incoming one arrives from where you are going. Both happen inside the same box, so the surface never appears to be rebuilt.

Files

Arrow keys move between tabs as well — the selection follows focus, and the direction of travel follows the keys.

Settings

Jump from here back to Activity and the movement reverses, because direction is state the transition is told about rather than a fixed animation.

Content expands from where it is

Revealed content grows out of the row that revealed it, at its own position, instead of appearing at full height somewhere below.

How is the height animated?
The content animates to block-size: auto directly, because the continuity layer turns on interpolate-size. Nothing is measured, no height is cached, and content that changes while open stays correct.
What happens on the way back?
Collapsing is the same transition in reverse. The content stays rendered while it shrinks rather than disappearing on the first frame, so closing is as readable as opening.

Surfaces come from what opened them

A menu is anchored to its trigger and scales out of its edge. A dialog expands away from the control you pressed rather than from the middle of the screen — open the two dialogs below from buttons at opposite ends of the row and watch where each one comes from.

States resolve rather than switch

Hover lifts, press sinks, focus rings fade in, marks grow out of their centre, and the switch thumb travels the track. Every one of these is a state the user can watch arrive.

Patterns

Components are the vocabulary; patterns are the sentences. Each of these is built only from what the system already ships — no new colors, no new shadows, no new spacing. If a pattern needs something the system does not have, that is a finding about the system, not a reason to write a one-off.

A row that continues past its frame

The edges fade instead of cutting, so the row reads as running underneath them, and each card snaps to the same place every time — moving left and right returns you to positions you have already seen. Scroll it with a trackpad or use the controls.
